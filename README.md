## Abstract

**Background:** Algorithmic bias threatens equitable AI deployment across critical domains including criminal justice, healthcare, and content generation, yet systematic quantification and effective mitigation strategies remain insufficiently documented.

**Methods:** We conducted a systematic review of empirical studies published 2016–2025, analyzing bias across three AI modalities: large language models, image generation systems, and video models. We evaluated mitigation techniques using standardized benchmarks (WinoBias, StereoSet, BBQ, FairFace) and developed an efficiency frontier framework comparing bias reduction, accuracy impact, and scalability.

**Results:** Empirical evidence reveals severe disparities: COMPAS demonstrates 1.91× false positive rate disparity (44.9% vs. 23.5%); facial recognition exhibits 43.4× error rate gaps (34.7% vs. 0.8%); text-to-image models show 84% gender bias in medical imaging; deepfake detection accuracy drops 49.8 percentage points (99.8% to 50%) in real-world deployment. Intersectional analysis reveals multiplicative rather than additive bias compounding. State-of-the-art mitigation achieves 40–70% bias reduction with minimal accuracy loss, with inference-time techniques (Chain-of-Thought: 70% reduction, 0% accuracy impact) dominating efficiency frontiers.

**Conclusions:** While mathematical impossibility theorems limit simultaneous satisfaction of multiple fairness criteria, scalable solutions exist through systematic integration of prompt engineering (rapid deployment), mechanistic interpretability (attention pruning), and participatory design. Critical research gaps include causal fairness at scale, multimodal bias propagation, and real-time adaptive debiasing for evolving societal norms.

**Keywords:** Algorithmic Fairness · Bias Mitigation · Large Language Models · Intersectionality · Constitutional AI · Systematic Review
